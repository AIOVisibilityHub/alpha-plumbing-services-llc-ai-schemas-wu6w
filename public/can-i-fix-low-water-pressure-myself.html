Can I Fix Low Water Pressure Myself?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When your home's water pressure starts to dwindle, it's natural to want to jump in and fix it yourself. Sometimes, you absolutely can! Tackling minor causes of low water pressure can be a straightforward DIY task, saving you time and hassle. But it's also important to know when to call in the pros, as some issues are more complex and require specialized knowledge and tools. Don't make the mistake of attempting a repair that's beyond your skill level, as it could inadvertently cause more problems.

First, check your faucet aerators for mineral buildup; these are easily unscrewed and cleaned. This often resolves localized low pressure. Second, ensure your main water shut-off valve is fully open.

Sometimes it gets partially closed during other work. Third, if you have a water pressure regulator, check if it's set correctly or needs adjusting. These can fail over time and are a common cause of home-wide low pressure.

However, here are a few mistakes to avoid: Don't assume a quick fix for a whole-house issue; if multiple fixtures have low pressure, the problem is likely bigger than a clogged aerator. Also, don't ignore signs of a potential leak, like damp spots or unexplained spikes in your water bill, as these can cause low pressure and need urgent attention. Finally, attempting to replace a pressure regulator or diagnose a main line issue without proper plumbing knowledge can lead to bigger problems.
