When to Replace Your Old Water Heater

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It’s a fact of life that even the best water heaters don’t last forever. While repairs can extend its life, sometimes replacement is the smarter, more cost-effective option. Knowing when to make that call can save you from unexpected breakdowns and costly emergency situations, especially if you're in the Goodyear area where we help a lot of folks with this.

One big indicator is age. Most conventional tank water heaters last about 8-12 years. If yours is older than that and starting to have issues, replacement is often a good idea.

Frequent repairs are another strong sign. If you're constantly calling a plumber for leaks, inconsistent heating, or other problems, the cost of repairs can quickly add up and exceed the cost of a new, more efficient unit. Decreased efficiency, shown by higher energy bills, also suggests it's time for an upgrade; newer models are much more energy-efficient.

And, of course, rust-colored water or visible leaks around the tank are critical signs that the inside of your tank is corroding and could fail catastrophically at any moment. You definitely don’t want a flooded garage or utility closet!

Making the decision to replace your water heater can feel like a big one, but it’s an investment in your home’s comfort and safety.
