Is It Time for a New Water Heater Installation?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Deciding whether to repair or replace your water heater can be a tricky call, but knowing when a new installation is truly the best path forward can save you from ongoing headaches and unexpected costs. A primary indicator is the age of your current unit. If your water heater is over 10-12 years old, especially a traditional tank model, it's likely nearing the end of its functional life.

Even if repairs are possible, their cost-effectiveness diminishes with age, and you'll likely face more issues soon after. Persistent leaks, particularly from the tank itself, are almost always a sign that a new installation is necessary. Tank leaks usually mean the internal lining has failed, which isn't typically repairable.

Another strong sign is frequent breakdowns or repairs. If you're constantly calling a plumber to fix the same issue, or if the repair costs are adding up to a significant portion of a new unit's price, replacement becomes the smarter financial choice. Also, if you're noticing rust-colored water, loud banging noises from the tank, or a significant decrease in hot water availability or temperature, these are all indicators that your water heater is struggling.

While repairs can offer a temporary fix, a new installation often provides greater efficiency, reliability, and peace of mind.
