Mistakes to Avoid When Fixing a Dripping Faucet

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Tackling a dripping faucet yourself can seem like a straightforward task, but it's easy to make a few missteps that can turn a simple repair into a bigger headache. Knowing what to watch out for can save you time, money, and frustration.

Here are some common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to Turn Off the Water Supply: This is a big one! Always, always turn off the water to the faucet you're working on. Failing to do so can lead to a messy flood, even a minor one, and potential water damage. You'll usually find the shut-off valves directly under the sink.
  • Using the Wrong Tools or Force: Plumbing parts can be delicate. Using pliers with too much force or the wrong type of wrench can strip screws, break components, or scratch your faucet's finish. Make sure you have the right tools for the job, and remember, gentle persuasion works better than brute force.
  • Not Replacing All Worn Parts: Often, people replace just the obvious worn washer. However, there might be other O-rings or seals that are also degraded and need replacing. A complete repair means checking all potential culprits.
  • Overtightening Components: While you want a tight seal, overtightening screws or nuts can damage washers, crack pipes, or warp parts, leading to more leaks down the line. Snug is good; straining isn't.
  • Ignoring the Type of Faucet: Not all faucets are created equal! Compression, cartridge, ceramic disc, and ball faucets all have different internal mechanisms. A repair for one won't work for another. Identify your faucet type before you begin so you know which parts you'll need.
