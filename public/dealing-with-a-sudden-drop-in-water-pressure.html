Dealing with a Sudden Drop in Water Pressure

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Nothing's quite as frustrating as stepping into the shower only to be met with a pathetic dribble of water. A sudden drop in water pressure throughout your home isn't just annoying; it's often a sign that something's amiss with your water line. It's one of those problems that can seem minor at first, but it points to a potential issue that you really shouldn't ignore.

First, quickly check if the issue is isolated to one faucet or affects all fixtures. If it's just one, it might be a clogged aerator or a problem with that specific fixture. But if every faucet, shower, and appliance is experiencing low pressure, your main water line is a prime suspect.

This could be due to a significant leak somewhere along the line, a blockage from mineral buildup, or even root intrusion if you have older pipes. Another possibility could be a faulty pressure regulator, but a compromised water line is a very common culprit for a whole-home pressure drop. Ignoring low pressure can lead to other problems, including inefficient appliance operation or even further damage if the underlying cause is a leak.

It’s always a good idea to have a professional plumber take a look when you're facing consistent low water pressure.
