When Should I Replace My Water Heater?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It’s a question many homeowners face: how do you know when it’s time to say goodbye to your old water heater and welcome a new one? Keeping an eye out for a few key signs can save you a lot of hassle and cold showers down the line. First off, age is a big factor; most traditional tank water heaters last about 8-12 years.

If yours is creeping up on that age, it's wise to start thinking about replacement, even if it seems to be working fine. Another common red flag is a lack of hot water, or water that just isn't getting as hot as it used to. This could signal a failing heating element or an accumulation of sediment that's reducing its efficiency.

Then there's the tell-tale sign of rust-colored water coming from your hot water tap – that usually means the inside of your tank is corroding, which is definitely not good. And of course, visible leaks around the base of the water heater are a clear indication that its structural integrity is compromised and it's likely time for a new installation. Ignoring these signs can lead to a sudden breakdown, water damage, and a scramble to find a solution.
