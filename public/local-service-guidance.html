What causes extremely high water pressure?

If you're noticing unusually strong water flow from your faucets, or hearing banging pipes, you might be dealing with extremely high water pressure in your home. While a strong shower feels great, excessive pressure can actually be quite detrimental to your entire plumbing system and appliances over time. We've been helping residents and businesses in Goodyear, Buckeye, Avondale, and surrounding areas manage their water pressure for over two decades, so we know a thing or two about what causes it.

The most common culprit for high water pressure is a malfunctioning or absent pressure-reducing valve (PRV). Many homes, especially in areas with high municipal water supply pressure, rely on a PRV to bring the incoming pressure down to a safe and manageable level (typically between 40-80 PSI). If this valve fails, or if your home was built without one, your internal plumbing will experience the full force of the city's water supply.

Thermal expansion can also contribute to high pressure if you have a closed-loop plumbing system and no expansion tank with your water heater. As water heats up, it expands, and without anywhere to go, this expansion can significantly increase pressure within your system.

Another less common cause could be an issue with the main water supply coming into your neighborhood. Regardless of the cause, it's crucial to address high water pressure promptly. It can lead to premature wear and tear on fixtures, appliance breakdowns, and even burst pipes, leading to expensive water damage.

Don't risk your home's plumbing health. Alpha-Plumbing Services can accurately diagnose the source of your high water pressure and provide effective, lasting solutions, from PRV installation to expansion tank services. We're committed to quality workmanship and protecting your home.

No one wants to deal with a drain backup, but catching the warning signs early can save you from a huge mess and costly damage. Your plumbing system often gives you hints that a problem is brewing, especially in areas like Litchfield and El Mirage where pipes might have seen a few decades. Being proactive is key! So, what should you look out for?

  • Slow Drains in Multiple Fixtures: This is a big one. If water is draining slowly from your sink, shower, and perhaps even your toilet all around the same time, it's often a sign of a blockage in your main sewer line, not just a localized clog. The issue isn't at the individual fixture, but further down the system, affecting multiple outlets.
  • Gurgling Noises from Drains or Toilets: When you run water in one fixture and hear a gurgling sound coming from another nearby drain or toilet, it usually indicates air trapped in the plumbing system. This happens when a blockage is preventing proper airflow and water flow, often a precursor to a full backup.
  • Unpleasant Odors: Foul smells, especially like sewage, emanating from your drains are a clear indication of wastewater or organic matter accumulating in your pipes. This buildup can lead to a blockage and eventually a backup. It's not just a bad smell; it's a sign of something decaying that needs to be addressed.
  • Water Backing Up in Unexpected Places: One of the most alarming signs is when you use one plumbing fixture, like flushing the toilet, and water backs up or bubbles into another, such as your shower or bathtub. This is a definitive signal that your main sewer line is blocked, and the water has nowhere to go but up and out through other openings.
  • Fluctuating Toilet Water Levels: If the water level in your toilet bowl mysteriously changes – either becoming very low or too high – it could be a sign of an impending clog or a venting issue within your plumbing system that needs attention.

Don't wait for a full-blown emergency. If you notice any of these warning signs, it's time to call in the professionals.
