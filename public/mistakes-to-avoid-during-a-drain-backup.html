Mistakes to Avoid During a Drain Backup

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When a drain backup hits, it's messy, stressful, and you just want it fixed – fast. But reacting impulsively can lead to bigger problems, costing you more time and money. Alpha-Plumbing Services, LLC, with our extensive experience in places like Glendale and Phoenix, wants you to avoid these common missteps.

  • Continuing to use plumbing fixtures: This is probably the biggest mistake. If your main drain is backed up, continuing to flush toilets, run water, or use appliances like washing machines will only add more wastewater to the system, causing it to overflow in other areas of your home. Immediately stop all water usage until the issue is resolved.
  • Ignoring the problem: Hoping it will just go away? Not a chance. Drain backups, especially main line ones, don't fix themselves. Ignoring them can lead to extensive water damage, structural issues, and exposure to harmful bacteria if sewage is involved. Act quickly to mitigate damage.
  • Using harsh chemical drain cleaners: As with recurring clogs, pouring caustic chemicals into a backed-up drain is a bad idea. They won't clear a significant backup and can create a dangerous chemical soup that's hazardous for anyone, including professional plumbers, who later tries to clear the drain manually. They can also corrode your pipes over time.
  • Over-plunging a serious backup: While a plunger can work for small, isolated clogs, trying to plunge a widespread drain backup, especially a main line issue, is largely ineffective and can sometimes push the clog further down or even damage the seals on your fixtures, creating more leaks.
  • Attempting DIY fixes without proper knowledge or tools: Unless you're a seasoned plumber with the right equipment (like a sewer snake or camera), trying to tackle a major drain backup yourself can be risky. You might damage pipes, get injured, or just make the problem worse, increasing the cost of professional repair.
