Unexpected Gas Smell Near Your Fire Pit? Here's What to Do

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Smelling gas near your outdoor fire pit is definitely a red flag, and it's not something you should ignore. Gas leaks can be dangerous, so acting quickly and safely is key. First off, if you detect that distinct sulfur-like odor of natural gas or propane, turn off the gas supply to your fire pit immediately.

You usually do this at a shut-off valve located near the fire pit or at your main gas meter. Don't try to locate the leak yourself or use any ignition sources like lighters or electrical switches. After you’ve turned off the gas, make sure everyone, including pets, moves to a safe area away from the fire pit.

It's smart to open up any nearby windows or doors if the smell has somehow drifted indoors, just to help ventilate the space. The next crucial step is to call in the professionals. Trying to fix a gas leak yourself, especially involving gas lines, is incredibly risky and can actually make things worse.
